Agentic AI (source: Diyi Yang, Stanford CS329Z)

Revision notes on AI agents: what an agent is and how it differs from an LLM workflow, a short history from the 1973 actor model to ReAct and AutoGPT, the anatomy of an LLM agent (LLM core, planning and reasoning, memory, tools and environment), multi-agent systems, tool use and MCP, and the open challenges of reliability, safety, privacy and evaluation. Each page covers one part of the lecture, with interactive demos and links to the exact moment in the video.

Credit. These notes follow Lecture 1 of Stanford CS329Z: Engineering AI Agents (Fall 2026), given by Prof. Diyi Yang (the course is co-taught with Michael Ryan and John Yang) and published free by Stanford Online (course site). The structure, examples and explanations are hers. These are my personal, unofficial notes; the demos and the orange "going beyond the lecture" boxes are my own, so check the lecture for the definitive treatment.

Related: the prompting, chain-of-thought and RL-for-LLMs pages in my Transformers notes (Stanford CS229) build up the reasoning models these agents run on.
