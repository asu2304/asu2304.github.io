Lecture 1 · A brief history of deep learning

Biological Neurons

Deep learning borrows one word from biology: neuron. A biological neuron collects signals through its dendrites, "decides" in the cell body whether the total is strong enough, and fires down its axon. Billions of them, wired in layers that each handle a more abstract job, turn light on the retina into "that is my friend Ram".

1 · The parts of a neuron

PartBiologyWhat the artificial neuron keeps
DendritesReceive signals from other neuronsThe inputs $x_1,\dots,x_n$
SynapsesThe point of connection; some connections are stronger than othersThe weights $w_1,\dots,w_n$
Soma (cell body)Processes the incoming informationA sum $\sum_i w_i x_i$
AxonSends the output signal onward (the neuron "fires")The output $y$, after a threshold

The analogy is deliberately loose. Prof. Khapra's own caveat: this is a cartoon of a neuron. Real neurons are far more complicated, and an artificial neuron is only inspired by one.

2 · A toy example: will I laugh at this joke?

Imagine a tiny network deciding whether you laugh. Some neurons respond to what you see, some to what you hear, some to what you have read. Each one fires if its own input is strong enough, and a final neuron fires (you laugh) if enough of them do. Try it.

Each sensory neuron fires when its input is above $0.5$. The output neuron adds up the three firing signals (each counts 1) and fires if the sum reaches the threshold $\theta$.

3 · Layers and the division of work

The brain is a massively parallel, interconnected network of roughly $10^{11}$ neurons. Neurons are arranged in a hierarchy, and each layer has a role. The visual cortex is the classic example:

  1. V1 detects edges and corners in what the retina sees.
  2. V2 groups edges into simple shapes and form.
  3. AIT (anterior inferior temporal cortex) recognises high-level objects: faces, for instance.

Information flows from simple to abstract, layer by layer. This picture (many simple units, arranged in layers, each layer building on the one below) is what deep learning copies. Every later idea in the course, from the perceptron to CNNs, is a version of it.

Going beyond the video. The figure of about $10^{11}$ neurons (≈86 billion) and roughly $10^{14}$–$10^{15}$ synapses is the usual estimate. Modern large networks have parameter counts in the same range of orders of magnitude as synapses, but a parameter is far simpler than a synapse, so the comparison is only a rough sense of scale.

Key takeaways

  1. A biological neuron: dendrites receive, synapses weight, the soma processes, the axon fires.
  2. An artificial neuron keeps the bones of this: inputs, weights, a sum and a threshold.
  3. The brain is a huge parallel network arranged in layers; each layer does a more abstract job (V1 → V2 → AIT).
  4. The analogy is a cartoon. It inspires deep learning; it does not describe the brain.
