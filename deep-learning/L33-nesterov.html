Nesterov Accelerated Gradient Descent

Look before you leap. Momentum's update has two parts, the history and the current gradient, and the history part is already known before we compute anything. So move by the history first, compute the gradient there, and let that look-ahead gradient decide the rest of the step. If the history is about to carry you past the minimum, the look-ahead gradient notices and pulls you back early: shorter U-turns, smaller oscillations.

1 · The question

Momentum converges faster than gradient descent, but it overshoots the minimum and comes back, again and again. Can we reduce these oscillations? Yes: Nesterov accelerated gradient (NAG). Its one-phrase summary: look before you leap.

2 · Two parts of one step

Recall momentum, written as one step:

$$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,u_t=w_t-\underbrace{\eta\beta\,u_{t-1}}_{\text{history: already known}}-\underbrace{\eta\,\nabla w_t}_{\text{gradient at }w_t} .$$

The current derivative is computed at the current weight $w_t$. But we are going to move by the history part anyway, and we already know it. So why not:

  1. Move by the history part first, to the partially updated point $w_{\text{look ahead}}=w_t-\eta\beta\,u_{t-1}$.
  2. Compute the derivative there, at the look-ahead point, instead of at $w_t$.
  3. Use that derivative to make the second part of the move.
$$u_t=\beta\,u_{t-1}+\nabla\big(w_t-\eta\beta\,u_{t-1}\big),\qquad w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,u_t$$

$\nabla(\cdot)$ means the derivative of the loss with respect to $w$, evaluated at whatever is inside the bracket. Same equations for $b$; $u_{-1}=0$. Compared with momentum, the only change is where the gradient is evaluated.

The lecture describes the look-ahead point as "$w_t$ minus the history". Since the step is $\eta\,u$, the history part of the move is $\eta\beta\,u_{t-1}$, so that is what I subtract; with the lecture's $\eta=1$ the two are identical.

3 · Why it helps: a 1-D picture

The lecture draws a loss curve $\mathscr{L}(w)$ and follows a few steps. Starting at $w_0$, the history accumulates and the steps grow: $w_1$, then $w_2$ further down. At $w_2$ the history says "go right" (we've been going down that way), and the slope at $w_2$ is negative, so the gradient also says "go right". Momentum adds both and takes a big step, landing far beyond the minimum.

NAG instead first moves by the history alone. That look-ahead point is already past the minimum, where the slope is positive, so its gradient says "go left". The second part of the step pulls back, and the effective $w_3$ ends up much closer to the minimum. It checks "have I already crossed the minimum?" before committing.

What the lecture points out in its animation:

  • Momentum (blue) takes a long U-turn; NAG, having looked ahead and realised it is overshooting, does not go the full distance and turns sooner.
  • Throughout the run, NAG's oscillations are shorter. It corrects its course quickly; momentum keeps going and only later realises it made a mistake.
  • Because momentum enters the valley fast and relies on the (large) gradient at its current point, it can shoot right out of the valley. NAG's partial update reveals that it is already crossing the valley, so it comes back instead. The chance of escaping the minimum valley is smaller.
Takeaway. Looking ahead lets NAG correct its course quicker than momentum, so its oscillations are smaller and it is less likely to escape the minimum valley, while keeping momentum's speed on flat regions.
Going beyond the video. NAG costs nothing extra: still one gradient per step, just evaluated at a different point. Deep-learning libraries implement it with a change of variables (tracking the look-ahead point itself), so the code looks like momentum with one extra term; e.g. PyTorch's SGD(..., momentum=0.9, nesterov=True).

Key takeaways

  1. Momentum's step = history part $\eta\beta u_{t-1}$ (already known) + gradient part.
  2. NAG: move by the history first, evaluate the gradient at $w_t-\eta\beta u_{t-1}$, then finish the step: $u_t=\beta u_{t-1}+\nabla(w_t-\eta\beta u_{t-1})$, $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta u_t$.
  3. If the history is about to carry you past the minimum, the look-ahead gradient points back and shortens the step.
  4. Result: shorter U-turns, smaller oscillations, less chance of escaping the valley, with the same speed on plateaus.
