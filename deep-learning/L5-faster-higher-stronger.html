Lecture 5 · A brief history of deep learning

Faster, Higher, Stronger

Once deep learning worked, the race was on to make it train faster and generalise better. Three lines of progress ran in parallel: better optimisation algorithms (Nesterov, Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam), better activation functions (from sigmoid and tanh to ReLU and its relatives) and better regularisation (dropout, batch normalisation). Together with GPUs and big datasets, they are why deep networks went from hard to routine.

1 · Better optimisation

  • 1983Nesterov accelerated gradient: look ahead along the momentum direction before computing the gradient.
  • 2011Adagrad: a separate learning rate per parameter, shrinking for parameters that have seen large gradients.
  • 2012RMSProp: Adagrad with a decaying average, so learning rates don't shrink to zero.
  • 2015Adam: momentum plus RMSProp-style scaling, with bias correction. Still the default for most networks.
  • 2016–18Further variants (Eve, AMSGrad and others) fix corner cases in Adam.

Going beyond the video. Plain gradient descent must keep $\eta<2/25$ here or the steep $w_2$ direction blows up, and with such a small $\eta$ the shallow $w_1$ direction crawls. Momentum averages out the zig-zag; Adam rescales each coordinate by its own gradient size, so both directions move at a similar pace. The course derives all of these properly in the optimisation lectures.

2 · Better activation functions

The non-linearity applied after each weighted sum changed too: logistic sigmoid → tanh → ReLU (rectified linear unit, $\max(0,z)$) → leaky ReLU, parametric ReLU, ELU, maxout, Swish and more. Sigmoid and tanh flatten out for large $|z|$, so their gradients vanish and deep networks stop learning; ReLU's gradient is exactly 1 for every positive input.

3 · Better regularisation

Deep networks have many parameters and can memorise. New regularisers kept them honest: dropout (2014) randomly switches off neurons during training; batch normalisation (2015) normalises each layer's inputs; plus data augmentation, weight decay and early stopping. All of these get their own lectures later.

Key takeaways

  1. Optimisers: Nesterov (1983) → Adagrad (2011) → RMSProp (2012) → Adam (2015) and beyond.
  2. Activations: sigmoid → tanh → ReLU family; non-saturating gradients make deep nets trainable.
  3. Regularisation: dropout, batch norm and others let huge networks generalise.
