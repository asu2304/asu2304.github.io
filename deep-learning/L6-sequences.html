Lecture 6 · A brief history of deep learning

The Curious Case of Sequences

Speech, text, music, video and time series are sequences: each element depends on what came before, and lengths vary. Recurrent networks handle this by feeding their own output back in. They were proposed in the 1980s, found to be very hard to train because gradients vanish or explode over long sequences, and rescued by the LSTM (1997), then GRUs, sequence-to-sequence models and attention (2014), and finally transformers (2017).

1 · Why sequences are different

  • Order matters: "dog bites man" ≠ "man bites dog".
  • Each element interacts with the others: the meaning of a word depends on the words around it; a frame of video on the frames before it.
  • Length varies: a feed-forward network expects a fixed-size input; a sentence can have 3 words or 300.

A recurrent network reuses the same weights at every time step and carries a state forward: $s_t=\sigma(U x_t + W s_{t-1} + b)$.

2 · The timeline

  • 1982Hopfield network: a recurrent network used as a content-addressable memory.
  • 1986Jordan network: the output at time $t$ is fed back as input at $t+1$.
  • 1990Elman network: the hidden state is fed back instead, the form used in modern RNNs.
  • 1991–94Vanishing and exploding gradients (Hochreiter 1991; Bengio, Simard and Frasconi 1994): training RNNs over long sequences is very hard.
  • 1997LSTM (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber): gates decide what to keep, forget and output, giving gradients a path that doesn't shrink.
  • 2014GRU (Cho et al.), sequence-to-sequence learning (Sutskever, Vinyals, Le) and attention (Bahdanau, Cho, Bengio) transform machine translation.
  • 2017Transformers ("Attention is all you need"): drop recurrence entirely, keep attention. The basis of today's large language models.

3 · See the gradient vanish

When you backpropagate through $T$ steps, the gradient reaching step $t-k$ is multiplied by roughly the same factor $k$ times: the recurrent weight times the slope of the activation. A factor below 1 shrinks it geometrically; above 1 blows it up.

Scalar RNN $s_t=\tanh(w\,s_{t-1}+x_t)$. The plot shows $|\partial s_T/\partial s_{T-k}|$ on a log scale against how many steps back, $k$. The gated curve mimics an LSTM cell, where the gradient is multiplied by the forget gate $f$ instead.

Real networks use matrices, where the largest singular value of $W$ (times the activation slope) plays the role of $w$. The proper derivation (backpropagation through time) is a later lecture.

Key takeaways

  1. Sequences: order matters, elements interact, lengths vary → recurrent networks.
  2. Hopfield (1982), Jordan (1986), Elman (1990) networks.
  3. Vanishing/exploding gradients made long sequences hard; LSTM (1997) gave a fix.
  4. 2014: GRU, seq2seq and attention; 2017: transformers.
