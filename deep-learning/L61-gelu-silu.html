From GELU to SiLU: Activations for Modern Networks

ReLU multiplies its input by 1 or 0 depending on the input's sign; dropout multiplies by 1 or 0 at random. GELU combines the two: multiply $x$ by a Bernoulli variable whose probability of being 1 grows with $x$, then take the expected value, $x\,\Phi(x)\approx x\,\sigma(1.702x)$. An automated search over activation functions later found the same shape, $x\,\sigma(\beta x)$, called Swish; with $\beta=1$ it is SiLU.

1 · Three functions that "multiply by 1 or 0"

GELU, the Gaussian error linear unit, is motivated by noticing a similarity between three things:

FunctionWritten asWhat decides 1 vs 0
Hard threshold$1$ if $x>0$, else $0$the sign of $x$
ReLU$1\cdot x$ if $x>0$, else $0\cdot x$the sign of $x$
Dropout mask$1\cdot x$ with probability $p$, else $0\cdot x$a coin toss

The functional form is the same; only the condition differs. ReLU brings the dependence on the input (like hard thresholding), and dropout brings stochasticity. Can we combine the two ideas?

2 · A stochastic ReLU

Define the activation as

$$f(x)=m\cdot x,\qquad m\sim\text{Bernoulli}\big(\Phi(x)\big).$$

So $m=1$ with probability $\Phi(x)$ and $m=0$ otherwise, and the probability itself depends on the input. To be precise about "$x$": in a hidden layer, neuron 21 has pre-activation $a_{21}$, and its activation is $h_{21}=f(a_{21})=m\cdot a_{21}$ with $m\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\Phi(a_{21}))$. The $x$ here is the neuron's pre-activation.

$\Phi$ must output a probability in $[0,1]$. Two natural choices:

  • the logistic function, $\Phi(x)=\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})$;
  • since we're in the world of probability, the CDF of the standard normal: $\Phi(x)=P(X\le x)$ for $X\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$. The bell-shaped density integrates to an S-shaped curve that goes from 0 at $-\infty$ to 1 at $+\infty$.

3 · Take the expectation

  1. $\mathbb E[f(x)]=\mathbb E[m\,x]$
  2. $=\Phi(x)\cdot(1\cdot x)+\big(1-\Phi(x)\big)\cdot(0\cdot x)$
  3. $=x\,\Phi(x)$

With the normal CDF, $\Phi$ has no closed form, but good approximations exist. One is the "nasty formula" on the slide; the other is much simpler:

$$\Phi(x)\approx\tfrac12\Big(1+\tanh\!\Big[\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\big(x+0.044715\,x^3\big)\Big]\Big)\qquad\text{or}\qquad \Phi(x)\approx\sigma(1.702\,x).$$

Both are very close to the true CDF. Taking the second:

$$\text{GELU}(x)=x\,\Phi(x)\approx x\,\sigma(1.702\,x)$$ A weird-looking formula on its own, but well motivated: it is the expected output of a ReLU-like gate that opens at random, more often for larger inputs.

4 · SELU: fixing zero-centring inside the activation

Plot ReLU, ELU and GELU together. None is zero-centred (recall from L59 why that matters). ReLU can take very large positive values but only 0 on the negative side. ELU is a bit better, with some mass below zero; GELU too. Still, all are tilted towards positive.

One remedy is normalization techniques (later in the course). Another is to build the normalization into the activation itself. That is the scaled exponential linear unit:

$$\text{SELU}(x)=\lambda\begin{cases}x & x>0\\ \alpha\,(e^x-1) & x\le0\end{cases}\qquad \lambda\approx1.0507,\ \ \alpha\approx1.6733 .$$

With the right scaling, the outputs become roughly balanced around zero. (The lecture quotes the two empirically derived numbers as "1.05 or 1.67"; they are not alternatives but the two constants: $\lambda\approx1.05$ and $\alpha\approx1.67$.)

5 · Searching for activation functions: Swish and SiLU

How long can we keep inventing these by hand? Could we search for them automatically? One line of work observed that activation functions are built from two kinds of operations:

  • unary (one input): $-x$, $\lvert x\rvert$, $a\cdot x$, $e^x$, …
  • binary (two inputs): $\max(x_1,x_2)$, $x_1\cdot\sigma(x_2)$, …

Restrict the search to a limited set of each, try many combinations, and keep what performs best. Exploring that space efficiently used ideas from reinforcement learning (beyond our scope). Many functions came out, some resembling the ELU/GELU family, and the conclusion was that any function of the form

$$\text{Swish}(x)=x\,\sigma(\beta x)$$

is a good activation function. Look at the special cases:

$\beta$FunctionName
learnable$x\,\sigma(\beta x)$Swish

6 · Which one should I use?

Recap of everything covered: sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, leaky ReLU, parametric ReLU, ELU, maxout, GELU, Swish (generalizing GELU) and SiLU (a special case). A Papers-with-Code plot of usage over time shows GELU rising with Transformers and ReLU staying popular for more than a decade.

ActivationWhere it's used today
ReLUMore or less the standard for convolutional networks; its variants exist but are less popular
GELUTransformers
tanhLSTMs and RNNs
sigmoidGenerally bad as a hidden activation, but whenever a value must lie in $(0,1)$ and act like a probability (gates, some attention functions)

This ends the activation functions. The fourth axis from the 2006–2009 investigations, weight initialization, comes next.

Going beyond the video. The lecture says GELU was introduced with the first Transformer (2017). In fact GELU was proposed by Hendrycks and Gimpel in 2016, and the original Transformer used ReLU. GELU became the Transformer default through BERT and GPT. Swish/SiLU came from Ramachandran, Zoph and Le (2017); SiLU had been proposed earlier (Elfwing et al.). Note that $x\sigma(1.702x)$ only approximates $x\Phi(x)$ (the gap is under about 0.02, as the demo shows), and PyTorch exposes both: nn.GELU() exact or approximate='tanh'. As $\beta\to\infty$, Swish tends to ReLU; at $\beta=0$ it is the line $x/2$.

Key takeaways

  1. ReLU is "$1\cdot x$ or $0\cdot x$ by the sign of $x$"; dropout is "$1\cdot x$ or $0\cdot x$ by a coin toss". GELU uses a coin whose bias depends on $x$: $m\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\Phi(x))$.
  2. Its expectation is $x\,\Phi(x)$ with $\Phi$ the standard-normal CDF, approximated by $x\,\sigma(1.702x)$ (or a tanh formula).
  3. ReLU, ELU and GELU aren't zero-centred; SELU scales ELU ($\lambda\approx1.05$, $\alpha\approx1.67$) to fix that inside the activation.
  4. An automated search over unary/binary compositions found $x\,\sigma(\beta x)$ (Swish); $\beta=1.702$ gives (approx.) GELU and $\beta=1$ gives SiLU.
  5. In practice: ReLU for CNNs, GELU for Transformers, tanh in RNNs/LSTMs, sigmoid where a 0–1 output is needed.
