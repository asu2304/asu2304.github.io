Why Transformers? The Sequential Bottleneck of RNNs

An RNN encoder is handed the whole sentence at once, yet it computes $h_1, h_2, \dots, h_T$ one after another. That recurrence is what makes the representations contextual, and also what makes them slow. Attention already has a parallel streak: at a fixed decoder step, all the weights $\alpha_{t1},\dots,\alpha_{tT}$ can be computed together. The aim of the coming videos is to keep attention and contextual representations while dropping the recurrence.

1 · Building blocks so far

The course has met three architectures, and each was understood through its basic building block. Once you understand the block, a deep, wide network is just many copies of it.

ArchitectureBuilding block
Feed-forward networka sigmoid (or other non-linear) neuron: weighted sum of inputs, then a non-linearity
Convolutional networkthe convolution operation (and max pooling)
Recurrent networkthe recurrence $h_t=f(h_{t-1},x_t)$
Attention-based RNNthe attention function on top of the recurrence

Transformers are built from self-attention and cross-attention layers. This video sets them up by looking at what is wrong with RNN-based models.

2 · The input arrives at once; the computation doesn't

Take translation of “I enjoyed the movie Transformers”. The whole sentence is given at one go; it does not trickle in one word at a time. But an RNN encoder still processes it sequentially:

  1. $x_1$ is the embedding of “I”. Compute $h_1=f(h_0,x_1)$.
  2. Only now can you compute $h_2=f(h_1,x_2)$, then $h_3$, $h_4$, $h_5$, one step at a time.

Why wait? Each word already has an embedding (from word2vec, fastText, GloVe, or simply a learnable parameter of the network). But that embedding is computed from a corpus, independently of this sentence. What we want is the representation of the word in the context of this sentence, and the yellow boxes $h_t$ provide that because they take input from the rest of the sentence.

A forward RNN only sees the left context. A bidirectional RNN/LSTM also runs right to left (“Transformers movie the enjoyed I”) and concatenates the two states, so the representation of “movie” sees both sides.

The good and the bad. Contextual representations are exactly what we want. Sequential computation is not: each step waits for the previous one, so there is no parallelism within a sentence. Wish: get the same contextual representations from a model that is not recurrent.

The decoder is a different story. It produces the first output word, which is fed in to produce the next, so its input is itself being generated one step at a time. Some sequential work there is unavoidable. The question is whether the encoder can be sped up.

3 · From one final state to attention

In the plain encoder–decoder, only the last state $h_5$ goes to the decoder, and the whole output is produced from that single vector. There is no notion of alignment: nothing says that when the decoder produces the first word of the translation it should focus on “I”.

The attention-based model fixes that. Run the encoder once, keep all its outputs $h_1,\dots,h_T$ (you can now throw the RNN away), and at every decoder step feed a fresh context vector (also called the thought vector):

$$e_{ti}=f_{\text{ATT}}(s_{t-1},h_i),\qquad \alpha_{ti}=\frac{\exp(e_{ti})}{\sum_{j=1}^{T}\exp(e_{tj})},\qquad c_t=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\alpha_{ti}\,h_i .$$

$\alpha_{ti}$ is the attention paid to input $i$ at output step $t$; the softmax makes each row a distribution. Stack the $h_i$ as columns of a matrix and $c_t$ is just that matrix times the vector $(\alpha_{t1},\dots,\alpha_{tT})$: a linear combination of the columns. The alignment function used earlier in the course was

$$f_{\text{ATT}}(s_{t-1},h_i)=V_{att}^{\top}\tanh\!\big(U_{att}\,s_{t-1}+W_{att}\,h_i\big).$$

Plot all the $\alpha_{ti}$ as a $T_1\times T_2$ heat map (input length by output length) and the bright cells show which input word each output word focused on. For a translation with nearly one-to-one word correspondences, the bright cells line up along the alignment.

4 · What can run in parallel?

Two questions lead straight to transformers.

  1. At a fixed step $t$, can $\alpha_{t1},\dots,\alpha_{tT}$ be computed in parallel? Yes. Each $e_{ti}$ needs only $s_{t-1}$ (already available) and $h_i$ (already available). Compute all scores at once, then normalise them at once. No matter how long the sentence, nothing waits on position $i-1$.
  2. Can the weights for all steps $t$ be computed in parallel? No. $\alpha_{4\cdot}$ needs $s_3$; $s_3$ needs the context $c_3$; $c_3$ needs all of $\alpha_{3\cdot}$. Unless $\alpha_{3\cdot}$ is done, $\alpha_{4\cdot}$ cannot start.
Attention is parallel within a time step but not across time steps, and the culprit is the recurrent connection through $s_{t-1}$. If we could get rid of recurrence and rely only on the part that parallelises, we'd have a new architecture.

5 · The wish list

RNN-based encoder–decoders with attention work very well (machine translation, summarisation, image and video captioning). The only complaint is that, for one training example, the sequence of computations cannot be parallelised. So:

  • Keep attention. It is what computes contextual representations, and it already parallelises within a time step.
  • Keep the idea behind recurrence: every word's representation should depend on the words around it.
  • Drop the computational cost of recurrence: no step should wait for the previous one.

The next video introduces the architecture that does this, from the paper “Attention Is All You Need”. For the history of how transformers took over, see L4.

Going beyond the video: parallel isn't free. Removing recurrence makes the number of sequential steps constant per layer, which is what GPUs reward. The price is that every word now compares itself with every other word, so the work per layer grows like $T^2$ rather than $T$. For sentences that is a great trade; for very long documents it is the main cost of transformers. My transformer revision notes go further.

Key takeaways

  1. Each architecture is understood through its building block; for transformers these are self-attention and cross-attention.
  2. An RNN encoder gets the whole sentence at once but computes $h_1,\dots,h_T$ sequentially. The recurrence buys contextual representations and costs parallelism.
  3. Attention gives a context vector $c_t=\sum_i\alpha_{ti}h_i$ per decoder step, adding the alignment that a single final state lacks.
  4. At a fixed $t$ all $\alpha_{ti}$ can be computed in parallel; across $t$ they cannot, because $\alpha_{t\cdot}$ needs $s_{t-1}$, which needs $c_{t-1}$.
  5. Goal: an architecture that keeps attention and contextual representations but has no recurrent connection.
