Self-Attention: Queries, Keys, Values and Scaled Dot-Product Attention

Every word embedding $h_i$ is turned into three vectors: a query $q_i=W_Qh_i$, a key $k_i=W_Kh_i$ and a value $v_i=W_Vh_i$. Word $i$'s query is dotted with every key to score how much each word matters to it; a softmax turns the scores into weights; the new representation $z_i$ is the weighted sum of the values. Stacked as matrices, this is a handful of matrix products, $Z=\text{softmax}(Q^\top K/\sqrt{d_k})\,V^\top$, so all $T$ outputs come out in parallel.

1 · One vector in, three vectors out

Take the embedding $h_1\in\mathbb R^{d}$ of the first word. Multiply it by three learnable $d\times d$ matrices:

$$q_1=W_Q\,h_1,\qquad k_1=W_K\,h_1,\qquad v_1=W_V\,h_1 .$$

These are the query, key and value vectors. Why three? The old attention function needed three vectors (L64), and now each word supplies all three. Do the same for every word, $h_1,\dots,h_T$: $3T$ vectors, all independent of one another, so all computable in parallel. $W_Q,W_K,W_V$ are the parameters this layer adds.

2 · Computing $z_1$

  1. Scores. Fix the query $q_1$ (the word of focus) and dot it with every key, including its own: $$e_{1j}=q_1^{\top}k_j,\qquad j=1,\dots,T .$$ The scoring function is simply the dot product.
  2. Weights. Softmax the score vector: $$\alpha_{1j}=\frac{\exp(e_{1j})}{\sum_{l=1}^{T}\exp(e_{1l})} .$$
  3. Output. Take the weighted sum of the values: $$z_1=\sum_{j=1}^{T}\alpha_{1j}\,v_j .$$

The roles match the old attention. There, $s_{t-1}$ stayed fixed while $h_j$ ran over the inputs: $s_{t-1}$ was effectively the query. Here $q_1$ stays fixed while the keys $k_1,\dots,k_T$ vary. And just as a third vector ($V_{att}$) sat outside the old formula, the values $v_j$ enter at the end. So $q$ and $k$ produce the weights $\alpha$, and $v$ is what gets averaged.

$z_2$ is the same story with $q_2$ as the query: $e_{2j}=q_2^\top k_j$, a softmax gives $\alpha_{2j}$, and $z_2=\sum_j\alpha_{2j}v_j$. Likewise up to $z_T$.

3 · Vectorising: all queries, keys and values at once

Computing $z_1$, then $z_2$, then $z_3$ would put us back in RNN territory. But each step is a matrix product. Put the embeddings as the columns of $H=[h_1,\dots,h_T]$:

$$Q=[q_1,\dots,q_T]=W_Q\,H,\qquad K=W_K\,H,\qquad V=W_V\,H .$$

With $d=64$ (the lecture's example), $H$ is $64\times T$ and $W_Q$ is $64\times64$, so $Q$ is $64\times T$. The projection could also map to a different size $d_1$ (bigger or smaller), giving $Q\in\mathbb R^{d_1\times T}$. What matters is that $T$ inputs give $T$ outputs, all at once.

4 · The whole output in one expression

$$\boxed{\,Z=\text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q^{\top}K}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V^{\top}\,}$$

Check the shapes (with $d=64$):

TermShapeMeaning
$Q^\top$$T\times64$row $i$ is $q_i^\top$
$K$$64\times T$column $j$ is $k_j$
$Q^\top K$$T\times T$entry $(i,j)$ is $q_i^\top k_j$: the attention matrix from L64's wish list
softmax (row-wise)$T\times T$entry $(i,j)$ is $\alpha_{ij}$
$V^\top$$T\times64$row $j$ is $v_j^\top$
$Z$$T\times64$row $i$ is $z_i^\top$

The lecture checks that the matrix version gives the same $z_2$ as the picture:

  1. Row 2 of $Q^\top K$ is $(q_2^\top k_1,\ q_2^\top k_2,\ \dots,\ q_2^\top k_T)$; after the softmax it is $(\alpha_{21},\dots,\alpha_{2T})$.
  2. Row 2 of a product $AV^\top$ is a linear combination of the rows of $V^\top$, weighted by row 2 of $A$.
  3. The rows of $V^\top$ are $v_1^\top,\dots,v_T^\top$, so row 2 of $Z$ is $\alpha_{21}v_1+\alpha_{22}v_2+\dots+\alpha_{2T}v_T=z_2$. ✓
Everything is parallel. $Q$, $K$ and $V$ are three matrix products; $Z$ is two more matrix products and a softmax. No $z_t$ waits for $z_{t-1}$. The output $Z$ holds the contextual representations $z_1,\dots,z_T$ of the inputs $h_1,\dots,h_T$.

5 · Scaled dot-product attention

The dot products are divided by $\sqrt{d_k}$, the square root of the key dimension ($d_k=64$ in the examples). The lecture notes that there is a justification but does not go into it; for practical purposes, you take the dot product and scale it. Hence the name scaled dot-product attention. As a block, one self-attention layer is:

H ──W_Q, W_K, W_V──▶ Q, K, V Q, K ──MatMul──▶ QᵀK (T×T) ──Scale ÷√d_k──▶ ──SoftMax──▶ α (T×T) α, V ──MatMul──▶ Z = α Vᵀ (T×64) = z₁ … z_T

Going beyond the video: why $\sqrt{d_k}$? If the components of $q$ and $k$ are roughly independent with mean 0 and variance 1, then $q^\top k$ is a sum of $d_k$ such products, so its variance is $d_k$ and its spread is $\sqrt{d_k}$. Large scores push the softmax into saturation: each row becomes almost one-hot, and the gradient through a saturated softmax is tiny, so learning stalls. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ brings the spread back to about 1 whatever the dimension. In the demo, untick the scaling and raise $d_k$: the score spread grows like $\sqrt{d_k}$ and the average largest weight heads to 1. With scaling on, it stays put. (The original paper writes the same formula with words as rows: $\text{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})\,V$.)

Key takeaways

  1. Each embedding $h_i$ yields $q_i=W_Qh_i$, $k_i=W_Kh_i$, $v_i=W_Vh_i$; the three matrices are learned.
  2. For word $i$: $e_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j$, $\alpha_{ij}=\text{softmax}_j(e_{ij})$, $z_i=\sum_j\alpha_{ij}v_j$. Query and keys make the weights; values are what is averaged.
  3. Stacked as matrices, $Q=W_QH$, $K=W_KH$, $V=W_VH$ and $Z=\text{softmax}(Q^\top K/\sqrt{d_k})V^\top$: $T$ outputs in parallel.
  4. $Q^\top K$ is the $T\times T$ attention matrix; row $i$ of $Z$ is exactly $z_i$.
  5. Scaling by $\sqrt{d_k}$ gives scaled dot-product attention: MatMul → Scale → SoftMax → MatMul.
