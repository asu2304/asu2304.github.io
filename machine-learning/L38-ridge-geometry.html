Lecture 38 · Regression: regularisation, geometrically

The Geometry of Ridge: Ellipses Meet a Circle

Ridge regression is the same as minimising the loss while keeping $w$ inside a circle around the origin. The loss grows in elliptical contours around the ML solution. The ridge solution is where the contours first touch the circle. That point is shorter than $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$, but generally has no exactly-zero coordinates.

Source: this lecture on YouTube (≈25 min). Previous: L37 · Next: L39.

1 · Ridge as a constrained problem

Two problems that turn out to have the same answer:

(A) Penalised (how we solve it)(B) Constrained (how we picture it)
$\displaystyle\min_{w}\ \sum_i(w^\top x_i-y_i)^2+\lambda\lVert w\rVert^2$$\displaystyle\min_{w}\ \sum_i(w^\top x_i-y_i)^2\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\lVert w\rVert^2\le\theta$

Equivalence (stated, not proved): for every $\lambda>0$ there is a $\theta$, depending on $\lambda$, such that (A) and (B) have the same optimal $w$. So the regulariser is really a constraint on where we search for $w$.

With two features (height and weight), $w=(w_1,w_2)$ lives in a 2-D parameter space. The constraint $w_1^2+w_2^2\le\theta$ is a disc centred at the origin with radius $\sqrt\theta$. The unconstrained $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$ can be anywhere, typically outside the disc.

2 · The shape of the loss: ellipses around ŵML

Write the loss as $f(w)=\lVert X^\top w-y\rVert^2$. $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$ has the smallest loss of all. Now ask: which $w$ have loss exactly $c$ more than the minimum, $f(w)=f(\hat w_{\text{ML}})+c$? Expanding both sides and simplifying (worth doing once yourself) gives

$$\big(w-\hat w_{\text{ML}}\big)^\top XX^\top\big(w-\hat w_{\text{ML}}\big)=c' .$$
  • If $XX^\top=I$, this is $\lVert w-\hat w_{\text{ML}}\rVert^2=c'$: a circle around $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$.
  • In general $XX^\top$ stretches that circle into an ellipse. Larger $c$ gives a larger ellipse, so the loss grows in concentric ellipses around $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$.

(Going beyond the video: the constant is exactly $c'=c$. The cross term $2(w-\hat w)^\top X(X^\top\hat w-y)$ vanishes because the normal equations say $X(X^\top\hat w_{\text{ML}}-y)=0$.)

3 · Where the ridge solution sits

Grow the ellipses outward from $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$, adding loss as you go. The first point where an ellipse touches the disc is the point inside the disc with the least loss. That's $\hat w_{\text{R}}$.

Parameter space $(w_1,w_2)$. Move $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$, change the ellipses' shape and tilt (that's what $XX^\top$ controls), and change the disc size $\sqrt\theta$ (a larger $\lambda$ means a smaller disc).

4 · What this shows

  • Ridge shrinks: $\hat w_{\text{R}}$ lies in the disc, so it's shorter than $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$. It pushes weights towards 0.
  • But it doesn't zero: the touching point is usually somewhere on the round edge, where neither coordinate is exactly 0. A weight that is exactly 0 would tell us the feature is useless and can be dropped. A weight that is merely small leaves us unsure whether to keep the feature.
The question this raises: the regulariser decides the shape of the region we search in. Could a different shape make the ellipses more likely to touch at a point where some coordinates are exactly 0? That's lasso, in L39.

Key takeaways

  1. Penalised ridge (λ) ⇔ constrained ridge ($\lVert w\rVert^2\le\theta$), for a matching θ.
  2. Loss contours are ellipses $(w-\hat w_{\text{ML}})^\top XX^\top(w-\hat w_{\text{ML}})=c'$ around $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$.
  3. $\hat w_{\text{R}}$ is where the growing ellipses first touch the disc: shorter than $\hat w_{\text{ML}}$, rarely with exact zeros.
