Lecture 42 · Classification: the simplest algorithm

k-Nearest Neighbours: Ask Your Neighbours

To label a new point, look at the training points closest to it and let them vote. One neighbour is fooled by outliers; everyone gives the same answer everywhere. The right k sits in between, found by cross-validation. The deeper problem: k-NN learns no model, so every prediction needs the whole training set.

Source: this lecture on YouTube (≈25 min). Previous: L41 · Next: L43.

1 · From one neighbour to k

  1. Nearest neighbour: given $x_{\text{test}}$, find the closest training point $x^\star$ and predict its label $y^\star$.
  2. Problem: if $x^\star$ is an outlier (say a mislabelled point inside the other class's region), you copy its mistake. Real datasets are noisy.
  3. Fix: ask $k$ neighbours $x_1^\star,\dots,x_k^\star$ and take the majority vote of $y_1^\star,\dots,y_k^\star$. If most of your neighbours agree, a single outlier can't flip the answer.

  • k = 1: the map is rugged, with holes around each outlier. That's the outlier sensitivity.
  • k = n: everyone votes on every point, so the answer is the overall majority everywhere: one colour fills the plane. Also bad.
  • In between there's a $k^\star$ that keeps the real structure (0 | 1 | 0) and ignores the outliers.

3 · Choosing k: cross-validation

$k$ isn't learned from the data; it's a hyperparameter. Smaller $k$ means a more complicated boundary. Pick $k$ by trying several and keeping the one with the best accuracy on held-out data. 4 · What's wrong with k-NN

IssueWhy it matters
Choosing a distanceWhy Euclidean? Different features may need different notions of "close". Try the three metrics above: the boundaries change. Domain knowledge can help design one.
Prediction is expensiveEvery prediction compares against all $n$ training points and picks the top $k$. With a billion points that's a billion distances, per prediction. The live counter above shows $n$, and the timer shows real milliseconds.
No model is learned (the root cause)Nothing is extracted from the data, so you can never throw the data away. Real learning should compress the data into a model that's enough for prediction, like the compression idea from the start of the course.

Key takeaways

  1. k-NN: predict the majority label of the $k$ closest training points.
  2. $k=1$ overfits (outlier holes); $k=n$ predicts the majority class everywhere. Choose $k$ by cross-validation.
  3. Open choices: the distance function.
  4. Biggest issue: no model, so prediction costs $O(n)$ distances per point and the data must be kept forever.
