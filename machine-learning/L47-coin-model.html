Lecture 47 · Classification: a learnable generative model

A Simpler Story: One Coin per Word

The dice story needed $2^{d+1}-1$ parameters. Keep the label coin, but replace each giant die with $d$ small coins, one per word: toss word $j$'s coin to decide whether that word appears. That drops the count to $2d+1$. The price is an assumption: given the class, the words are independent of each other.

Source: this lecture on YouTube (≈18 min). Previous: L46 · Next: L48.

1 · The new story

  1. Label, as before: toss a coin with $P(y=1)=p$. One parameter was never the problem.
  2. Features, changed: go to the room for that label. It holds $d$ coins, one per dictionary word. Coin $j$ in the spam room lands heads with probability $p^1_j$; in the non-spam room, $p^0_j$. Toss all $d$ coins: heads = the word appears, tails = it doesn't.

Notation: in $p^y_j$ the superscript is the label and the subscript is the word. Two sets of coins are needed because the same word behaves differently by class: "lottery" might have $p^1_j=0.8$ in spam but $p^0_j=0.1$ otherwise.

2 · The probability of an email

Because the coins are tossed independently, the probability of a whole email given its class is a product, one factor per word. Write the email as $x=[f_1,\dots,f_d]$ with $f_i\in\{0,1\}$:

$$P\big(x=[f_1,\dots,f_d]\,\big|\,y\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\big(p^y_i\big)^{f_i}\big(1-p^y_i\big)^{1-f_i}$$

The exponents just pick the right factor: if word $i$ is present ($f_i=1$) the factor is $p^y_i$; if absent, $1-p^y_i$. It's the Bernoulli likelihood from the estimation lectures, once per word.

4 · What we gave up: class-conditional independence

Suppose an email is spam and you learn it contains "lottery". Does that change your belief that it also contains "money"? In real spam, yes: the two travel together. In this model, no: "money" has its own coin, and the lottery coin's result doesn't touch it.

Class-conditional independence assumption: given the class, the features are independent of each other. Knowing the label is all that matters; knowing one word tells you nothing more about another. The independence is conditional on the class: it holds inside the spam room and inside the non-spam room, not across the whole inbox.

Key takeaways

  1. Story: label coin $p$; then $d$ word coins from the room for that label, $p^1_j$ or $p^0_j$.
  2. $P(x\mid y)=\prod_i (p^y_i)^{f_i}(1-p^y_i)^{1-f_i}$, and $P(x,y)=P(y)\,P(x\mid y)$.
  3. Parameters: $2d+1$ instead of $2^{d+1}-1$.
  4. The cost is the class-conditional independence assumption: given the label, words don't inform each other.
