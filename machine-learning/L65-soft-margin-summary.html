Lecture 65 · Support vector machines: summary

Soft-Margin SVM: The Full Picture

Read the complementary-slackness conditions from the primal side: a point with margin below 1 has $\alpha^*_i=C$; a point with margin above 1 has $\alpha^*_i=0$; a point exactly on the margin line can have anything in $[0,C]$. Together with L64 this gives a complete map. The upshot: the soft-margin SVM is kernelisable, has simple box constraints, and is still sparse, which is why it works so well in practice.

Source: this lecture on YouTube (≈18 min). Previous: L64 · Next: L66.

3 · Sparsity survives

Only points on a supporting hyperplane or on the wrong side of it can have $\alpha^*_i>0$. All points comfortably beyond their margin line, usually the great majority, have $\alpha^*_i=0$ and can be discarded. If the data really is separable-with-some-outliers (in the kernel's feature space), the support vectors remain a small fraction of the data. The soft margin loses nothing on sparsity.

4 · Why the soft-margin SVM is so widely used

  1. Kernelisable: the dual depends on the data only through $X^\top X$, so non-linear structure is handled by a kernel.
  2. Simple constraints: $0\le\alpha\le C$, easy even for projected gradient methods.
  3. Sparse solutions: few points have $\alpha^*_i\ne0$, so prediction is cheap.
  4. Handles outliers through the slack variables and $C$.

It is a quadratic optimisation problem, and a great deal of work has gone into solving it fast; modern solvers run in time linear in the problem's size. It has been especially successful on structured data (data with features in a table).

5 · SVM or logistic regression?

Logistic regression (L56) also copes with outliers and also has a kernel version. The two are well-motivated in different ways and differ subtly in what they optimise, but in practice they are usually competitive. The usual advice: try both, compare on a validation set, keep the winner.

Key takeaways

  1. Margin $<1\Rightarrow\alpha^*_i=C$; margin $>1\Rightarrow\alpha^*_i=0$; margin $=1\Rightarrow\alpha^*_i\in[0,C]$.
  2. Support vectors: on a supporting hyperplane or on its wrong side. Everything else is irrelevant to $w^*$.
  3. Soft-margin SVM = kernels + box constraints + sparsity + outlier tolerance.
  4. In practice it is on a par with logistic regression; pick by validation.
