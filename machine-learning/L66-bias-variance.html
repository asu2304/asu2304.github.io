Lecture 66 · Ensembles: the motivation

Overfitting, Underfitting and the Bias–Variance Trade-off

Labels are structure plus noise. A model that is too simple treats real structure as noise and underfits: it is stable but systematically wrong (high bias). A model that is too flexible treats noise as structure and overfits: it fits the training data perfectly but changes wildly from one dataset to the next (high variance). Both are "weak learners", and ensemble methods are ways of turning weak learners into strong ones.

2 · Weak and strong learners

A weak learner is a classifier that is only somewhat better than random on test data. For binary labels, coin-tossing gets 50%; a weak learner might get 55% or 60%. The goal of an ensemble is to convert weak learners into a strong one. Learners are typically weak for one of two opposite reasons.

4 · Variance: how much the model changes with the data

Draw a fresh dataset from the same distribution: the noise is random, so the points shift a little. Refit the same kind of model.

  • The overfit model changes a lot, because each time it chases that dataset's noise. At a fixed test input, its predictions land all over the place. It is too sensitive to the data: high variance.
  • The underfit model barely moves: the best line for one dataset is close to the best line for another. Low variance. But it keeps missing the truth in the same way.

5 · Bias: how well the assumed structure can match the truth

Bias measures how far the assumed structure is from the real one. A line can never follow a sine curve: high bias. A degree-8 polynomial can imitate it closely: low bias, paid for with high variance.

The trade-off. Error is always a mix of bias and variance. Simple model classes: error dominated by bias. As complexity grows, bias falls and variance rises. Somewhere in between is the best balance.

Bias and variance, measured

For each degree: 200 datasets, 200 fits. Bias² is how far the average fit is from the truth; variance is how much the fits scatter around their average (both averaged over inputs).

6 · Hyperparameters are trade-off dials

Every hyperparameter we cross-validated was balancing bias against variance by choosing how large a class of models to search:

7 · What comes next

Two ways to deal with the trade-off by combining classifiers: one starts from learners that overfit (high variance) and tames them; the other starts from learners that underfit (high bias) and strengthens them. These are bagging and boosting.

Key takeaways

  1. Labels = structure + noise. Underfitting mistakes structure for noise; overfitting mistakes noise for structure.
  2. Overfit models: zero training error, high variance (very sensitive to the dataset).
  3. Underfit models: stable, but high bias (the assumed structure can't match the truth).
  4. Error mixes bias and variance; complexity trades one for the other. Hyperparameters ($k$, depth, $\lambda$, $C$) set the balance.
  5. Ensembles turn weak learners of either kind into strong ones.
