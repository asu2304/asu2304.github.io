Generation, Temperature and the Training Loss

To generate, sample a token from the softmax, append it, and feed the longer sequence back in. Dividing the logits by a temperature keeps their order but changes how peaked the distribution is: $\tau\to0$ is greedy decoding, $\tau>1$ explores the tail. Training is simply minimising the negative log-likelihood, a sum over positions of $-\log$ the probability given to the token that actually came next.

1 · Autoregressive generation ▶ 26:24

Suppose the model $\theta$ is trained. Generation (also called decoding) produces $x_1,x_2,x_3,\dots$ in order:

$$x_t \sim \operatorname{softmax}\big(f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_{t-1})\big).$$
  1. Start from the BOS token $x_0$; run the transformer; sample $x_1$ from the softmax.
  2. Feed $x_1$ back in; run again; sample $x_2$.
  3. Keep going: each sampled token becomes part of the next input.
That feedback loop is why it's called autoregressive. When computing a likelihood you already have the whole sequence and only ask how probable it is. When generating you start with nothing and must produce the sequence yourself.

2 · Starting from a prompt ▶ 28:10

Usually you don't generate from nothing. The model is given a prompt $x_1,\dots,x_k$ (a question, an instruction, …) and completes it: sample $x_{k+1}$ from $\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_k))$, then $x_{k+2}$ the same way, and so on.

3 · Temperature ▶ 29:02

When generating you needn't follow the model exactly. Divide the logits by a temperature $\tau$ (sometimes written $T$) before the softmax:

$$x_t \sim \operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{\tau}\,f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_{t-1})\Big).$$

$f_\theta$ is a $|V|$-dimensional vector of logits. Dividing by $\tau>0$ does not change their order (bigger ones stay bigger) but changes their scale.

The lecture's worked example ▶ 30:49

Three tokens with logits $(2,\,1,\,-1)$.

  1. $\tau=1$. $$\operatorname{softmax}(2,1,-1)=\frac{(e^{2},\,e^{1},\,e^{-1})}{e^2+e^1+e^{-1}}=\frac{(7.389,\,2.718,\,0.368)}{10.475}\approx(0.705,\,0.259,\,0.035).$$ The lecturer guessed "maybe 0.7 and 0.3": ordered, but the mass is not all on token 1.
  2. $\tau=0.01$ (almost 0). The logits become $(200,\,100,\,-100)$, and $$\operatorname{softmax}=\frac{(e^{200},\,e^{100},\,e^{-100})}{e^{200}+e^{100}+e^{-100}}.$$ $e^{200}$ is astronomically bigger than $e^{100}$, so the other terms are negligible: the result is $\approx(1,\,0,\,0)$.
  3. Conclusion. Small $\tau$ amplifies the logits and sharpens the distribution towards a deterministic one: you are effectively taking the max instead of sampling.

4 · Top-k sampling ▶ 35:18

There are other sampling schemes (more in the course's lecture notes). One: the real vector has $|V|$ (≈250k) entries, not 3. Keep only the top $k$ tokens, drop all the others, renormalise, and sample from those $k$. The top $k$ may not cover all the probability mass, but you avoid generating junk tokens from the far tail.

Going beyond the lecture: top-p (nucleus) sampling keeps the smallest set of top tokens whose probabilities add up to at least $p$, so the number kept adapts: few when the model is confident, many when it isn't. It's included in the demo below.

5 · Training: minimise the negative log-likelihood ▶ 36:12

For one example $x_1,\dots,x_T$ the loss is the negative log-likelihood:

  1. $$-\log p_\theta(x_1,\dots,x_T) = -\log\big[p_\theta(x_1)\,p_\theta(x_2\mid x_1)\cdots p_\theta(x_T\mid x_1,\dots,x_{T-1})\big]$$
  2. $= -\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_1,\dots,x_{t-1})$ (log of a product is a sum)
  3. $$= -\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\big[\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_{t-1}))\big]_{x_t}$$

Here $x_t$ is the particular token seen in the data: the softmax gives a probability for every token, and we pick out the entry indexed by $x_t$, a single scalar. Take its log and sum over $t$.

$$\mathcal L(\theta) = -\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\big[\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_{t-1}))\big]_{x_t}$$ Minimise it with an optimiser such as SGD or Adam (an extension of SGD), called as a black box.

A student asked whether this only trains the probability of the last token. No: the sum runs over every $t$ from 1 to $T$, so every position contributes its own next-token prediction.

6 · What comes next ▶ 38:57

Everything so far treats $f_\theta$ as a black box. The rest of the lecture (the lecturer warns it's complicated and may take more than 30 minutes) opens it: which operations turn the embeddings into the logits.

Key takeaways

  1. Generation samples $x_t\sim\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(x_0,\dots,x_{t-1}))$ and feeds it back; with a prompt you start after $x_k$.
  2. Temperature divides the logits: order unchanged, sharpness changed. $(2,1,-1)$ gives $\approx(0.70,0.26,0.04)$ at $\tau=1$ and $\approx(1,0,0)$ at $\tau=0.01$.
  3. $\tau=0$ is greedy and deterministic; $\tau>1$ adds diversity.
  4. Top-k keeps the $k$ likeliest tokens and renormalises.
  5. Training minimises $-\sum_t\log[\operatorname{softmax}(f_\theta(x_{<t}))]_{x_t}$ over every position, with SGD or Adam.
