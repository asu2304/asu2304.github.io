Transformers and Large Language Models (source: Tengyu Ma, Stanford CS229)

Revision notes on large language models: tokenisation and the transformer, efficient attention, mixture of experts, prompting and fine-tuning, then the reinforcement learning behind modern reasoning models, from policy gradient to PPO and RL with verifiable rewards. Each page covers one part of a lecture, with interactive demos and links to the exact moment in the video.

Credit. These notes follow four lectures from Stanford CS229: Machine Learning (Spring 2026), given by Tengyu Ma (the course is co-taught with Chris Ré) and published free by Stanford Online (course site). The structure, examples and explanations are his. These are my personal, unofficial notes; the demos and the orange "going beyond the lecture" boxes are my own, so check the lectures for the definitive treatment.

Video titles: on YouTube, the second to fourth of these videos are titled “Lecture 16: Basic Concept in RL, Policy Gradient” and “Lecture 18/20: GMM (EM), PCA”, which don't match their content. These notes name each lecture by what it teaches. For a different take on the same architecture, see Prof. Mitesh Khapra's transformer lectures in my Deep Learning notes (L63–L67).

Lecture 1 · Large language models and the transformer

Full video: Lecture 14: Transformers, In-Context Learning (Stanford Online).

  1. L1.1Tokenisation: Why subwords beat characters and words, vocabularies and token IDs, and greedy byte-pair encoding video
  2. L1.2Autoregressive model: The |V|^T problem, chain-rule factorisation, embeddings, BOS and logits, and causality video
  3. L1.3Decoding: Autoregressive sampling, prompts, temperature, top-k, and the negative log-likelihood loss video
  4. L1.4Attention: Per-position MLPs, queries, keys and values, softmax-weighted values, and why not a giant MLP video
  5. L1.5Attention at scale: Matrix form, causal masking with −∞, multi-head attention, residuals and norms, T² cost and flash attention video

Lecture 2 · Efficient attention, mixture of experts, prompting and fine-tuning

Full video: Lecture 16: Basic Concept in RL, Policy Gradient (Stanford Online).

  1. L2.1KV cache and GQA: Recap of queries, keys and values, why the KV cache limits batch size, grouped-query attention, and √d_h scaling video
  2. L2.2MQA and sliding windows: Sharing keys and values rather than queries, multi-query attention, sliding-window reach, and QK-norm video
  3. L2.3Mixture of experts: Routing, reading 30B-A3B, top-k routing with renormalised weights, shared experts, and load balancing video
  4. L2.4In-context learning: Few-shot prompts with hidden labels, zero-shot task descriptions, GPT-3 and the industry shift, and emergence video
  5. L2.5Instruction tuning: Instruction data and loss, SFT vs instruction tuning, human-written chains of thought, and fine-tuning with little data video

Lecture 3 · Reinforcement learning basics and policy gradient

Full video: Lecture 18: GMM (EM), PCA (Stanford Online).

  1. L3.1Sequential decisions: Why greedy fails, exploration vs exploitation, learning from reward, and the MDP's states, actions and transitions video
  2. L3.2Rewards and returns: Reward functions, reward shaping, discounting, the M/(1−γ) bound, and the full MDP tuple video
  3. L3.3Policies and values: The Markov property, deterministic vs randomised policies, V^π, V* and π* video
  4. L3.4Bellman equation: The geometric-series recursion, the step-by-step derivation for V^π, and solving it as a linear system video
  5. L3.5Policy gradient: Stochastic parameterised policies, the objective η(θ), why ∇ can't go inside, and the log-derivative trick video
  6. L3.6REINFORCE: Trajectory log-probabilities, why the dynamics drop out, the final estimator, and sampling vs enumeration video

Lecture 4 · Policy gradient, PPO, and RL for reasoning LLMs
